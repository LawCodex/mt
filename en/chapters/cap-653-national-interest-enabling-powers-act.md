# National Interest (Enabling Powers) Act (Cap. 653)

**CHAPTER 653**

**NATIONAL INTEREST (ENABLING POWERS) ACT**

AN ACT to enable the implementation of certain treaties and measures and the restriction of trade and travel where the national or international interest of Malta so requires and to enable effect to be given to certain provisions of the Charter of the United Nations.

14th November, 2025

ACT XXXV of 2025.

## 1. Short title and scope.

(1) The short title of this Act is the National Interest (Enabling Powers) Act.

(2) The scope of this Act is to regulate the implementation of restrictive measures, including but not limited to domestic restrictive measures, United Nations Security Council Resolutions and European Union restrictive measures, and to implement the requisites and, or transpose:

(a) Directive (EU) 2024/1226 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 April 2024 on the definition of criminal offences and penalties for the violation of Union restrictive measures and amending Directive (EU) 2018/ 1673;

(b) Regulation (EU) 2024/886 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 March 2024 amending Regulations (EU) No. 260/2012 and (EU) 2021/1230 and Directives 98/26/EC and (EU) 2015/2366 as regards instant credit transfers in euro; and

(c) Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 31 May 2023 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets and amending Directive (EU) 2015/849 (recast).

## 2. Interpretation.

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires:

"Board" means the Sanctions Monitoring Board established in article 15;

"Charter" means the Charter of the United Nations signed in San Francisco on the 26th June 1945;

''Council of the European Union'' means the Council established by the Treaty;

"designated person, entity or body" means a natural or legal person, entity or body subject to European Union or domestic restrictive measures or restrictive measures resulting from a United Nations Security Council Resolution;

"domestic restrictive measures" means restrictive measures adopted by order in article 4;

"economic resources" means assets of every kind, whether tangible or intangible, movable or immovable, which are not funds but may be used to obtain funds, goods or services;

"European Union" means the European Union as referred to in the Treaty;

"European Union restrictive measures" means restrictive measures adopted by the European Union on the basis of Article 29 of the Treaty of the European Union or Article 215 of the Treaty of Functioning of the European Union;

"freezing of funds" means preventing the move, transfer, alteration, disposing or use of or access to funds, or dealing with funds in any way that would result in any change in their volume, amount, location, ownership, possession, character, designation or in a change that would enable the funds to be used, including portfolio management;

"freezing of economic resources" means preventing the use of economic resources to obtain funds, goods or services in any way, including but not limited to, by selling, hiring, disposing or mortgaging them;

"funds" means financial assets and benefits of every kind, including but not limited to:

(a) cash, cheques, claims on money, drafts, money orders and other payment instruments;

(b) deposits with financial institutions or other entities, balances on accounts, debts and debt obligations;

(c) publicly-traded and privately-traded securities and debt instruments, including stocks and shares, certificates representing securities, bonds, notes, warrants, debentures and derivatives contracts;

(d) interest, dividends or other income on or value accruing from or generated by assets;

(e) credit, right of set-off, guarantees, performance bonds or other financial commitments;

(f) letter of credit, bills of lading, bills of sale;

(g) documents showing evidence of an interest in funds or financial resources;

(h) crypto assets as defined in Article 3(1), point (5) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 31 May 2023 on markets in crypto-assets, and amending Regulations (EU) No. 1093/2010 and (EU) No. 1095/2010 and Directives 2013/36/EU and (EU) 2019/1937;

"instant credit transfer" means a credit transfer in euro which is executed immediately, twenty-four (24) hours a day and on any calendar day;

"international treaty" includes any bilateral or regional treaty;

"legal person" means any entity, including but not limited to, a body or association of persons, whether corporate or incorporate registered in Malta;

"Minister" means the Minister responsible for foreign affairs;

"parent company" shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in the Companies Act;

"payment account" shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in the Financial Institutions Act;

"Regulation (EU) 2016/679" means the Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation);

"Security Council" means the Security Council established under Chapter V of the Charter;

"United Nations" means the international organisation set up by the Charter;

"United Nations Security Council Resolution" means any Council Resolution which from time to time may be published by the United Nations Security Council imposing restrictive measures of whatever nature, referred to in the yearly compendiums of United Nations Security Council resolutions and decisions and includes any amendment thereto, including any amendment or addition to any related list of designated persons or entities and any repealing Resolution.

## 3. Applicability of the Act.

(1) This Act shall apply to legal persons, entities and bodies registered in Malta, natural persons resident in Malta, to citizens of Malta wherever they may be, or to vessels, aircraft or other means of transport registered in or belonging to Malta or travelling to or from such countries as the Minister may by means of regulations determine.

(2) Legal persons, entities or bodies as referred to in sub-article (1) having branches or majority-owned subsidiaries established in third countries where restrictive measures are not aligned with this Act shall ensure that those branches or majority-owned subsidiaries implement the provisions of this Act in so for as that third country legislation permits the implementation of such provisions:

Provided where legal persons, entities or bodies have branches or majority-owned subsidiaries established in third countries, and the legislation of such third countries does not permit the implementation of the restrictive measures established under this Act, legal persons, entities or bodies shall ensure that those branches and majority-owned subsidiaries apply additional measures to effectively handle the risk of violations of restrictive measures and the circumvention of any restrictive measures.

## 4. Domestic restrictive measures and power of the Minister to make regulations.

(1) Whenever the Security Council, acting in accordance with Article 41 of Chapter VII of the Charter, calls upon Member States of the United Nations to apply any measures to give effect to any decision of the said Security Council, provided that they are measures not involving the use of armed force under Article 42 of the Charter, or whenever an entity or competent authority appointed or set up by, or under the Charter advises, recommends, directs or orders Member States of the United Nations to take such action as may be advised, recommended, directed or ordered by such entity or competent authority, the Minister may, subject to the provisions of sub-article (4), make regulations as he may deem necessary or expedient for the implementation of such measures or action.

(2) Whenever the House ratifies or authorises the ratification of an international treaty or whenever such ratification or authorisation is made or given by an Act of Parliament, the Minister may, subject to the provisions of sub-article (4), make regulations as he may deem necessary or expedient to give effect to any of the provisions of such treaty or to give effect to any advice, recommendation, direction or order of any entity or competent authority appointed or set up by or under any such international treaty and any such regulations shall contain the text of the Resolution of the House ratifying or authorising the ratification of that treaty or shall contain a reference to the Act of Parliament by which the ratification is made or authorisation given, as the case may be.

(3) Whenever the Minister considers that the national or international interests of Malta so require, he may by regulations under this Act, impose a trade embargo with other countries, either in general or with any country or countries or part thereof, or the prohibition of travel to and from any country or countries or part thereof, as may be stated in the regulations.

(4) (a) Whenever the Minister considers that the national or international interests of Malta so require, he shall, on the recommendation of the Board, issue regulations under this Act to impose domestic restrictive measures against any person, entity or body and, orders:

(i) that any person, entity or body immediately freeze, without prior notice, all funds and, or economic resources of a designated person, entity or body;

(ii) that any person, entity or body immediately freeze, without prior notice, all funds and economic resources that are owned or controlled, whether wholly or jointly, directly or indirectly, by a designated person, entity or body, or funds and economic resources that are derived or generated from other funds or economic resources that are owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by a designated person, entity or body, and funds and economic resources of any person, entity or body acting on behalf of or at the direction of a designated person, entity or body;

(iii) that no Maltese citizen or any person, entity or body located in Malta shall make available any funds, economic resources or other related services, directly or indirectly, wholly or jointly, to or for the benefit of a designated person, entity or body or a legal person owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by a designated person, entity or body, or a natural person or legal person acting on behalf of, or at the direction of, a designated person, entity or body unless licensed or authorised for such purpose;

## 6. Direct applicability of United Nations Security Council Resolutions.

(1) For the purposes of this Act, United Nations Security Council Resolutions imposing or applying restrictive measures of whatever nature, or which advise, recommend, direct or order Member States of the United Nations to take such action as may be advised, recommended, directed or ordered, whether published before or after the coming into force of this article, shall be automatically binding in their entirety in Malta and shall be part of the domestic law thereof.

*Cap. 249.*

(2) The provisions of article 3(4)(e) of the Interpretation Act shall, mutatis mutandis, also apply to United Nations Security Council Resolutions referred to in this Act and to any amendment thereto and any repealing Resolution, and the reference in the said article, to publication in the Official Journal shall, in the case of United Nations Security Council Resolutions, be construed as a reference to publication in the yearly compendiums referred to in article 2.

## 7. Performance of obligations.

(1) It shall be prohibited to conclude transactions and assume new obligations the performance whereof contradicts the European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures implemented under this Act.

(2) It shall be prohibited to perform, until the European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures are in force, the transactions and obligations the performance whereof contradicts the Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures.

(3) Transactions entered into and obligations arising prior to the establishment of the implementation of European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures shall be terminated without delay unilaterally or by agreement between the parties or their performance must be suspended during the period of the implementation of the restrictive measures.

*        Cap. 16.*

(4) Persons, entities or bodies shall not be subject to civil liability for non-performance of transactions and obligations due to the implementation of European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures, or shall be exempted from civil liability in accordance with the procedure established in the Civil Code.

(5) The losses incurred by persons, entities or bodies as a result of lawful actions of the Board in implementing this Act shall not be compensated.

## 8. Realisation of economic resources.

(1) Economic resources, including those in transshipment, frozen in the implementation of European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures may be realised in accordance with the procedure established by the Board, where:

(a) the economic resources are perishable or at risk of deterioration, decay, damage or depreciation in value;

(b) the expense of keeping the economic resources is excessive or disproportionate to its fair market value when one also takes into consideration the time likely to determine whether the economic resources shall be subject to forfeiture or to a confiscation order;

(c) the economic resources are subject to a loan, mortgage or any other right over the economic resources or to taxes due; or

(d) there is any other valid and justifiable reason for the sale.

(2) Where a temporary administrator has been appointed in accordance with the procedure laid down in article 34, he shall take a decision on the realisation of economic resources.

(3) The proceeds of any sale pursuant to this article shall be held in an account maintained by the Board in the name of the designated person, entity or body at the Central Bank of Malta and shall remain in that account until the designation of the person, entity or body is no longer in effect.

## 9. Offences.

(1) Where it is intentional, the following breach of a prohibition of European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures shall constitute a criminal offence under this Act:

(a) making funds or economic resources available directly or indirectly to, or for the benefit of, a designated person, entity or body;

(b) failing to freeze funds or economic resources belonging to or owned, held or controlled by a designated person, entity or body;

(c) enabling designated natural persons to enter into, or transit through the territory of Malta;

(d) entering into or continuing transactions with a third State, bodies of a third State or entities or bodies directly or indirectly owned or controlled by a third State or by bodies of a third State, including the award or continued execution of public or concession contracts;

(e) trading, importing, exporting, selling, purchasing, transferring, transiting or transporting goods, as well as providing brokering services, technical assistance or other services relating to those goods:

Provided this shall constitute an offence, even if committed with gross negligence;

(f) providing financial services or performing financial activities;

(g) providing services other than those referred to in paragraph (f);

(h) circumventing European Union Restrictive Measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions or domestic restrictive measures by:

(i) using, transferring to a third party, or otherwise disposing of, funds or economic resources directly or indirectly owned, held, or controlled by a designated person, entity or body, which are to be frozen pursuant to European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions or domestic restrictive measures, in order to conceal those funds or economic resources;

(ii) providing false or misleading information to conceal the fact that a designated person, entity or body is the ultimate beneficial owner or beneficiary of funds or economic resources or has control over the said funds or economic resources which are to be frozen pursuant to European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions or domestic restrictive measures;

(iii) failure by a designated natural person, or by a representative of a designated entity or body, to comply with an obligation that constitutes European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions or domestic restrictive measures to report to the Board funds or economic resources within the jurisdiction of Malta, belonging to, owned, held, or controlled by them;

(iv) failing to comply with an obligation that constitutes a European Union restrictive measure or a restrictive measure adopted by United Nations Security Council resolution or domestic restrictive measures to provide the Board with information on funds or economic resources or information held about funds or economic resources within the territory of Malta, belonging to, owned, held or controlled by designated persons, entities or bodies and which have not been frozen, where such information was obtained in the performance of a professional duty;

(i) breaching or failing to fulfil conditions under authorisations granted by the Board to conduct activities, which in the absence of such an authorisation amount to a violation of a prohibition or restriction that constitutes European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures.

(2) The following conduct does not constitute a criminal offence and is deemed to be an administrative failure subject to the procedure established in accordance with article 14:

(a) conduct listed in sub-article (1)(a), (b) and (h) where that conduct involves funds or economic resources of a value of less than ten thousand euro (€10,000);

(b) conduct listed in sub-article (1)(d), (e), (f), (g) and (i) where that conduct involves goods, services, transactions or activities of a value of less than ten thousand euro (€10,000):

Provided that in determining whether the ten thousand euro (€10,000) threshold is met or otherwise with regards to the conduct referred to in paragraphs (a), (b), and (d) to (i) regard shall be had to any series of conduct that is linked and of the same kind committed by the same offender.

(3) Nothing in sub-article (1) shall be understood as imposing an obligation on legal professionals to report information that they receive from, or obtain on, one of their clients in the course of ascertaining the legal position of such client or performing the task of defending or representing that client in, or concerning, judicial proceedings, including providing advice on instituting or avoiding such proceedings.

(4) Nothing in sub-articles (1) and (2) shall be understood as criminalising humanitarian assistance for persons in need or activities in support of basic human needs provided in accordance with the principles of impartiality, humanity, neutrality and independence and, where applicable, with international humanitarian law.

## 12. Special mechanism for out-of-court settlement in case criminal proceedings have not yet been initiated.

(1) (a) Notwithstanding any other provision of this Act or any other law, where the Board is satisfied that there has been a breach of any provision of this Act and, or any regulations made thereunder, by any person, entity or body corporate which breach shall constitute a criminal offence and in the event that:

(i) after having regard to the particular circumstances, it is appropriate to impose an administrative penalty in accordance with this article; and

(ii) where the particular person, entity or body corporate has not previously been convicted of any breach of this Act and, or any regulations made thereunder, or which has not previously made use of this provision under this article or of the provisions of article 13 in the last five (5) years.

(b) As an alternative to criminal proceedings, the Board may, after a written request is made to him by the person, entity or body, hereinafter referred to as "person concerned", enter into an agreement with the person concerned, who has committed an offence against this Act and, or any regulation made thereunder, and upon the conclusion of an agreement made in accordance with this article between the Board and the person concerned to provide for the payment of an administrative penalty in addition to any sanction or measure, or the combination of other sanctions and measures which the Board deems appropriate in the circumstances of the case, the criminal liability of the person concerned for any breach against this Act and for any breach connected with the acts in connection with the agreement entered into, shall be extinguished when the terms of the agreement are fulfilled in their entirety by the person concerned:

Provided that the administrative penalty which such person concerned shall be liable to pay in respect of such breach shall not exceed half of the maximum administrative penalty which the person concerned would have been liable for had he been convicted of an offence against this Act and, or any regulations made thereunder:

Provided further that the person concerned shall make a request in writing to the Board for the agreement to be made. Following such a request by the person concerned, the Board shall within one (1) month of being notified by registered post of the request, inform the person concerned of the outcome of such request and in case the Board accedes to such request, it shall also notify the person concerned with the draft agreement:

Provided further the mentioned period of one month (1) may be extended once, unilaterally by the Board for a period of one (1) month, where the Board shall inform the person concerned of its decision while explaining in detail the reason on the basis of which this extension is made. The notification for the extension shall be made by registered mail in the first twenty (20) days from the day that the Board has been notified through the request of the person concerned according to sub-article (1).

(2) The notice in accordance with sub-article (1) shall specify:

(a) the date and nature of the alleged breach;

(b) a summary of the facts on which the allegation of a breach is based:

Provided that the summary shall be sufficiently detailed so as to fully and fairly inform the person of the allegation against him;

(c) any other matter, not consisting of previous convictions, which the Board deems relevant in the imposition of any administrative penalty and, or sanction or measure, or the combination of sanctions and measures which the Board deems appropriate;

(d) a draft agreement under this article which shall include:

(i) details of the breaches which shall include inter alia admissions made by the person concerned;

(ii) an obligation on the person concerned to pay the administrative penalty due and, if applicable, any sanction or measure, or the combination of sanctions and other measures, which the Board deems appropriate in the circumstances of the case;

## 16. Powers and functions of the substitute of the Chairperson.

In the Chairperson’s absence or inability to act as such, the Minister shall appoint a substitute from amongst the members of the Board.

## 17. Meetings of the Board.

(1) The meetings of the Board shall be called by the Chairperson on his own initiative or at the request of any two (2) of the other members and shall meet as often as may be necessary or expedient, but in no case less frequently than twice (2) in each year.

(2) The Board shall not act unless a quorum consisting of the Chairperson or his substitute in accordance with article 16 and not less than two (2) other members are present.

(3) The meetings of the Board shall be chaired by the Chairperson or in his absence by his substitute appointed in accordance to article 16.

(4) Senior officials from the Directorate referred to in article 15(4) shall be present at the meetings to assist the Board members as necessary.

(5) The decisions of the Board shall be adopted by a simple majority of the votes of the members present and voting and in the event of an equality of votes the Chairperson shall have and exercise the casting vote.

(6) Any vacancy among the members of the Board and any participation therein by a person not entitled to do so shall not invalidate the proceedings of the Board.

(7) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Board may regulate its own procedure.

(8) The Chairperson may, following consultation with Board members, invite observers on the Board as and when required provided they have the necessary security clearance.

(9) All acts done by any person acting in good faith as a member of the Board shall be valid as if he were an eligible member notwithstanding that some defect in his appointment or qualification be afterwards discovered.

## 18. Decisions in cases of emergency.

In cases of emergency, decisions shall be taken by at least two (2) members of the Board, one of whom shall be the Chairperson or his substitute appointed in accordance with article 16.

## 19. Board ruling.

(1) A person, entity or body may request in writing the Board to give a ruling on whether a proposed immediate action intended by him is prohibited by any particular domestic restrictive measures or any regulations made under this Act, or by any European Union restrictive measures, or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions or domestic restrictive measures. No liability shall result from any such regulations or European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolutions where a person, entity or body has acted in conformity with any such ruling.

(2) The Board may refuse to give a ruling on any matter not within the parameters of article 3.

(3) The Board reserves the right not to reply taking into account considerations related to the national interest.

(4) If the applicant considers that the Board’s ruling, decisions or denial for an authorisation are a result of an error of fact, the applicant may request the Board to reconsider its ruling or denial, as the case may be.

(5) A request for reconsideration shall be made within five (5) working days from the notification of the ruling, decisions or denial for an authorisation of the Board and may not be made concurrently with an appeal. The request for reconsideration shall include a written document containing the reasons for such a request. This is without prejudice to the right of appeal before the Sanctions Implementation Appeals Board.

(6) An appeal from the reconsideration issued under sub-article (1), may be filed by means of an application before the Sanctions Implementation Appeals Tribunal by the person, entity or body who had requested the Board to reconsider its decision and who feels aggrieved by the Board’s decision in relation to the said request.

## 20. Sanctions Implementation Appeals Tribunal.

(1) The Tribunal shall be composed of a Chairperson and two (2) other members appointed by the Minister.

(2) The Chairperson of the Tribunal shall be an advocate with a minimum of seven (7) years legal experience and shall have the qualifications, experience and skills, in particular in the field of restrictive measures required to fulfil his role.

(3) The other two (2) members referred to in sub-article (1) shall be persons who are well versed in the subject-matter and who have the qualifications, experience and skills, in particular in the field of restrictive measures, required to fulfil their roles.

(4) The Chairperson and other members of the Tribunal shall hold office for a period of three (3) years and shall be eligible for re-appointment for another term.

*   Cap. 12.*

(5) A member of the Tribunal shall be disqualified from hearing an appeal in such circumstances as would disqualify a judge in accordance with Sub-Title II of Title II of Book Third of the Code of Organization and Civil Procedure and in any case the member shall be substituted by another person appointed by the Minister.

(6) A member of the Tribunal may be removed from office by the Minister, on grounds of gross negligence, conflict of interest, incompetence or acts or omissions unbecoming a member of the Tribunal, proven misbehaviour or proven inability to perform the functions of his office, whether arising from infirmity of body or mind or any other cause.

(7) The Minister shall also designate a person to serve as Secretary to the Tribunal.

(8) An applicant aggrieved by any ruling, including those decisions taken in accordance with article 35 or denial for authorisation, may file an appeal against such a ruling, including those decisions taken in accordance with article 35 or denial for authorisation, and the Tribunal shall have jurisdiction to hear and determine such appeals.

## 21. Registry of the Tribunal.

(1) There shall be a Registry of the Tribunal and all records shall be filed therein.

(2) The Secretary of the Tribunal shall be responsible for managing the Registry. The Secretary shall also perform any other duty which may be assigned to him under this Act or any regulations made thereunder.

## 22. Procedure.

(1) An appeal as referred to in article 19(6) shall be filed in the Registry of the Tribunal within twenty (20) working days from service of the decision taken by the Board on the person, entity or body who had requested the Board to reconsider its decision.

(2) An appeal to the Tribunal may be filed on the following grounds:

(a) a material error as to the facts has been made;

(b) there was a material procedural error;

(c) an error of law has been made; and

(d) there was some material illegality, including unreasonableness or lack of proportionality.

(3) An appeal application shall be filed in the Registry of the Tribunal and shall contain:

(a) the grounds on which such appeal is based;

(b) the names of the witnesses the appellant intends to produce in evidence, together with their full address, stating in respect of each of them the facts and proof he intends to establish by their evidence;

(c) any other documents as may be necessary in support of the appeal.

(4) The Registry of the Tribunal shall, not later than ten (10) working days from the date of filing of the appeal, serve a copy of the appeal on the Board and request the Chairperson of the Board to file a reply, together with any other information on which such decision was based and which the Board considers relevant to the case, within twenty (20) working days from the date when the appeal application was served.

(5) Upon the expiry of the time-limits for filing the Board’s statement, the Tribunal shall appoint a date and time for the hearing. The Tribunal shall notify such date and time to the appellant and to the Chairperson of the Board.

(6) The parties to the appeal may be represented or assisted by their advocates or legal procurators, or by any person who enjoys their trust:

Provided that no person shall claim fees for representing or assisting a party before the Tribunal unless such assistance is provided by an advocate or legal procurator.

## 23. Competence of the Tribunal.

(1) The Tribunal shall be competent to hear and decide any appeal made to it in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

(2) The Tribunal may summon any person to appear before it to give evidence and produce documents. The Tribunal may also appoint experts to advise it on any technical issue that may be relevant to its decision. On the day appointed by the Tribunal for the hearing of the appeal, it shall hear under oath, the evidence of the appellant and of the Board, as well as of other witnesses. The Secretary of the Tribunal shall issue the necessary summons for any person involved in the hearing of the appeal.

(3) The Tribunal shall hold its sittings in public unless having regard to the nature of the appeal or any other matter before it, it deems it proper to conduct the proceedings or part thereof in camera.

(4) The Tribunal shall have the same powers as are competent to the First Hall, Civil Court according to law.

(5) Without prejudice to the provisions of this Act or any other law, the Tribunal shall regulate its own procedure.

## 24. Decisions of the Tribunal.

(1) In determining an appeal the Tribunal may:

(a) dismiss the appeal and confirm the decisions, ruling or denial for authorisation in its entirety;

(b) substitute the decisions, ruling or denial for authorisation;

(c) modify or vary the decisions, ruling or denial for authorisation; or

(d) annul the decisions, ruling or denial for authorisation.

(2) The Tribunal shall give reasons for its decision and shall cause such decision to be made public, omitting, if it deems it appropriate for reasons of confidentiality, the identity of the persons, entities or bodies involved.

(3) The decisions of the Tribunal shall be served to all parties to the appeal.

## 31. Freezing measures.

(2) Where the Board has reasonable grounds to believe that economic resources are owned or controlled by a designated person, entity or body, it shall issue a freezing measure, attaching the said economic resources in the hands of third parties for which there is a reasonable cause to believe that the economic resources are subject to freezing:

(a) freezing, without delay or prior notice, all funds and economic resources due or in pertaining or belonging to the designated person, entity or body, or that is wholly or jointly owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by the designated person, entity or body;

(b) freezing, without delay or prior notice, all funds and economic resources that are derived or generated from funds and economic resources that are owned or controlled, directly or indirectly by the designated persons, entities or bodies;

(c) freezing, without delay or prior notice all funds and economic resources of natural persons and legal persons acting on behalf of or under the direction of the designated person, entity or body;

(d) prohibiting the transfer, conversion, movement, pledging, hypothecating, mortgaging or otherwise disposing of any funds and economic resources in accordance with paragraphs (a) to (c);

(e) prohibiting any Maltese citizen or any person, entity or body located in Malta from making available any economic resources or funds or other related services, directly or indirectly, wholly or jointly, to or for the benefit of a designated person, entity or body; or an entity or body owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by a designated person, entity or body; or a person, entity or body acting on behalf of, or at the direction of, a designated person, entity or body, unless licensed or authorised in accordance with the relevant European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures.

(4) The freezing measure as mentioned in sub-article (1) shall become operative and binding on all third parties immediately upon the publication of European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by a United Nations Security Council Resolution or domestic restrictive measures mentioned in sub-article (1), and the said measure shall remain in force:

(a) in the case of a measure made by regulations in accordance with article 4(4)(a), until it is revoked according to the provisions of article 4(4)(a)(v); and

(b) in the case of a European Union restrictive measures or restrictive measures adopted by United Nations Security Council Resolution, until the de-listing of the designated person, entity or body is made by the United Nations Security Council or by the Council of the European Union, as the case may be.

(5) Where any funds are or become due to a designated person, entity or body from any person, entity or body while a freezing measure in accordance with this article is in force, such funds shall, unless otherwise directed in such measure, be deposited in a bank to the credit of the designated person, entity or body.

(6) When such measure ceases to be in force as provided in sub-article (2), a note of cancellation of the registration of such measure shall be entered in the Public Registry.

## 32. Obligations of persons, entities or bodies.

(1) The persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I shall:

(a) identify the identity of a customer and, where applicable, identify and verify the ownership and control structure of the said customer prior to engaging to service the same customer, and ensure on an on-going basis that the information so obtained is still current and up-to-date;

(b) regularly check the list of designated persons, entities or bodies in accordance with European Union restrictive measures, restrictive measures by the United Nations Security Council Resolutions and domestic restrictive measures and to screen their client databases against those lists prior to engaging with a client, on a regular basis and immediately after a change to any of these lists occurs and upon awareness that the relevant circumstances surrounding the client and its operations have changed;

(c) have in place and effectively implement internal controls, policies and procedures to ensure compliance with the obligations arising from this Act and European Union restrictive measures, restrictive measures by the United Nations Security Council Resolutions and domestic restrictive measures, with the said internal controls, policies and procedures having to be in compliance with Chapter IV of Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 31 May 2023 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets and amending Directive (EU) 2015/849 when being implemented by a payment service provider or a crypto-asset service provider when performing transfers of funds and crypto-assets respectively;

(d) immediately notify the Board in case targeted funds and economic resources are identified, and of the actions taken in relation to such funds and economic resources in compliance with the requirements of this Act, including in relation to any attempted transactions; and

(e) upon awareness, immediately notify the Board of any suspected violations of European Union restrictive measures, restrictive measures by the United Nations Security Council Resolutions and domestic restrictive measures.

(2) The Minister may, from time to time, by regulations amend Schedule I as necessary.

(3) All persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I may rely on a third-party to comply with their obligations in accordance with sub-article (1), with the person, entity or body relying on third parties remaining ultimately responsible for compliance with the said obligations.

(4) All persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I relying on other third parties to comply with their obligations in accordance with sub-article (1) shall take adequate steps to ensure that, upon request, such third-party shall immediately provide any relevant data, information and documentation required in terms of sub-article (1).

(5) All persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I operating in Malta shall take appropriate steps, proportionate to the nature and size of their business, to identify and assess the risks of violations of restrictive measures and proliferation financing and circumvention thereof that arise out of its activities or business. These steps shall take into account risk factors, including those relating to clients, geographical areas, products, services, transactions and delivery channels:

Provided that crypto-asset service providers shall in particular consider the risks associated with transfers of crypto-assets directed to or originating from a self-hosted address measure to mitigate and manage the risk of non-implementation and evasion of targeted financial restrictive measures and proliferation financing-related targeted financial restrictive measures.

(6) All persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I referred to in sub-article (5) shall properly document the risk assessment referred to in that sub-article and shall make such documentation available to the Board or any other relevant supervisory authority acting on the Board’s behalf, upon demand by the said Board or supervisory authority.

(7) All persons, entities or bodies listed in Schedule I shall ensure that the risk assessment carried out in terms of sub-article (5) is regularly reviewed and kept up-to-date.

(8) Any person, entity or body listed in Schedule I referred to in sub-article (1) shall be prohibited from informing the client or any third party in advance that a freezing order pursuant to this Act is to be applied.

(9) Any person, entity or body listed in Schedule I referred to in sub-article (1) shall retain records to demonstrate its compliance with the obligations referred to in accordance with this article for a period of five (5) years from the end of the business relationship with the clients which shall start to run as follows:

(a) in relation to the records retained to demonstrate compliance with the obligations referred to in accordance with sub-article (1)(a), from the date when the person, entity or body no longer services the client;

(b) in relation to the records to be retained to demonstrate compliance with the obligations referred to in accordance sub-article (1)(b), from the date when the verification takes place;

(c) in relation to the records to be retained to demonstrate compliance with any other obligation, from the date when the person, entity or body referred to no longer falls within any of the categories listed in Schedule I:

Provided that the Board may extend the five (5) year retention period in so far as that period is limited to what is strictly necessary in relation to the purpose for which the information is processed.

*Cap. 527.*

(10) In accordance with the provisions of the Protection of the Whistleblower Act, the Board, in any investigating, prosecuting, judicial or administrative authority shall protect and keep confidential the identity of any person, entity or body who reports, either internally within a person, entity or body or to the Board, awareness or suspicions that economic resources are the proceeds of violations of restrictive measures or the circumvention of the restrictive measures.

(11) Detrimental action shall be not taken against any person, entity or body, including employees or officials of the person, entities or bodies who reports, either internally within the person, entity or body or to the Board, awareness or suspicions of sanctions violation or sanctions circumvention or awareness or suspicions that funds are the proceeds of violations of restrictive measures or the circumvention of restrictive measures.

## 33. Obligations of payment service providers.

In the case of payment service providers offering instant credit transfers, they shall verify whether any of their payment service users are persons, entities or bodies subject to European Union restrictive measures. Such verifications shall be carried out immediately after the entry into force of European Union restrictive measures and immediately after the entry into force of any amendments of such European Union restrictive measures and at least once every calendar day:

Provided that during execution of an instant credit transfer, the payer’s payment service provider and the payment service provider involved in the execution of such instant credit transfer shall not verify whether the payer or the payee whose payment accounts are used for the execution of that instant credit transfer are persons, entities or bodies subject to European Union restrictive measures:

Provided further that is without prejudice to actions taken by the payment service providers in order to comply with European Union restrictive measures, other than designated persons, entities or bodies under European Union restrictive measures.

## 34. The establishment of a firewall mechanism.

(1) The Board shall have the right to appoint a temporary administrator to a legal person when European Union restrictive measures or any restrictive measure adopted by a United Nations Resolution or domestic restrictive measures are implemented in Malta in respect of the same legal person, the owner or controlling person thereof and where temporary administration is necessary to avoid adverse social, economic, ecological or other significant consequences for the public or Malta.

(2) A person, entity or body may be appointed as temporary administrator and such person shall be of good repute. A temporary administrator may be a natural person provided he is of age and legal capacity and not under any legal impediment to so act. A temporary administrator may be a legal person provided he is of good standing and the objects of which include the administration of legal persons.

(3) The legal person placed under temporary administration shall propose its temporary administrator to the Board for authorisation. The Board shall assess the appropriateness of the proposed temporary administrator, taking into consideration qualifications and experience, any conflicts of interest with the legal person to be administered, and other factors which may affect the proper performance of the activities.

(4) A temporary administrator shall be appointed by a decision of the Board. The Board shall determine the term of appointment of the temporary administrator, powers, functions and actions that the temporary administrator may perform only subject to prior approval of the Board. The decision of the Board may also provide, where necessary, that the powers of certain or all bodies of the legal person are suspended and temporarily taken over by the temporary administrator or that certain decisions of the bodies of the legal person require the temporary administrator’s approval.

(5) The conditions of activities of a temporary administrator shall be defined in a temporary administrator’s contract between the temporary administrator and the legal person as authorised by the Board. This contract shall also specify the temporary administrator’s remuneration and other expenses, including expenses related to the services provided by service providers, taking into account the scope of activities of the temporary administrator, his qualification and duration of activities, the procedure for reimbursement of the expenses as well as the liability of the temporary administrator, the procedure for his resignation and removal. Remuneration shall be paid, and expenses shall be reimbursed, including expenses related to services rendered by service providers from funds of the legal person put under temporary administration.

(6) A temporary administrator shall be appointed by the Board for a maximum period of one year. In exceptional cases, the time limit for the appointment of the temporary administrator may be extended by a decision of the Board. The temporary administrator may be replaced or removed from office at any time and if the need for his appointment ceases to exist, he shall be removed from office without delay.

(7) A temporary administrator shall have the right to engage service providers including legal, accounting, auditing and management consultants at his discretion to fulfil his functions.

(8) A temporary administrator shall take all required steps to ensure compliance with the requirements established in this Act and to avoid the adverse consequences referred to in sub-article (1). If necessary, the temporary administrator shall have the right to open deposit accounts with financial or credit institutions that operate in Malta which would be used to ensure the continuity of the activities of the legal person.

(9) The Board may require a temporary administrator to provide, within the time limits laid down in the contract referred to in sub-article (7), information on the financial situation of a legal person and on the economic activity carried out during the period of the appointment of the temporary administrator.

(10) A temporary administrator shall perform his functions prudently and in good faith. A temporary administrator shall not exercise the rights granted to him for his personal needs or those of third parties.

(11) A temporary administrator shall have the right to resign in accordance with the procedure established in the contract referred to in sub-article (5). If a temporary administrator resigns, the legal person placed under temporary administration shall without delay initiate the process for appointment of a new temporary administrator.

## 35. Administrative penalties.

(1) Any person, entity or body who breaches the provisions of article 32, and when such breach is not accompanied by any other breach of this Act, shall be liable to an administrative penalty to be imposed by the Board of not less than one hundred euro (€100) and not more than five hundred euro (€500) in respect of every separate breach:

Provided that in the case of payment service provides and crypto-assets providers, every natural person who, at the time of the commission of the breach, was a member of the management body of the relevant service provider shall be liable to the same administrative penalty as the legal person where the said natural person, through an act or omission, whether intentional or due to gross negligence, including through the lack of proper oversight of subordinates, caused or contributed to the commission of any such breach.

(2) The administrative sanction penalty shall be of not less than three hundred euro (€300) and not more than one thousand five hundred euro (€1,500) in respect of breaches that are deemed to be serious, repeated or systematic:

Provided that instead of or in conjunction with the imposition of any administrative penalty in accordance with sub-article (1) or this sub-article, the Board may issue directives in writing requiring any natural person or legal person to take any action or measure to remedy such breach or ensure compliance with the provisions of article 30(1):

Provided further that in cases where any natural or legal person fails to comply with any such directives issued in writing within the time and in the manner stipulated therein, the Board shall impose an administrative penalty in accordance with sub-article (1).

(3) The Board shall, in accordance with its policies and procedures, impose administrative penalties in accordance with this article, which penalties may be imposed either as a one-time penalty or as a daily cumulative penalty, or both.

(4) Administrative penalties imposed on a daily cumulative basis, shall not be less than a hundred euro (€100) and the accumulated administrative penalty shall not exceed two thousand five hundred euro (€2,500).

(5) Where the Board imposes an administrative penalty on a natural person or legal person, it shall inform in a timely manner any relevant supervisory authority or any other authority, body or committee responsible for the authorisation, licensing, registration or regulation of, or the granting of a warrant to, the person, entity or body concerned, and shall provide all relevant information on the administrative penalty which it deems necessary:

Provided that where the Board imposes an administrative penalty as stipulated in accordance with sub-articles (1) or (2), the Board may also recommend to the relevant supervisory authority, body or committee responsible for the authorisation, licensing, registration or regulation of, or the granting of a warrant to, the natural person or legal person concerned, to take any action or regulatory measures available to it in accordance with the applicable law as it may deem appropriate based on the specific facts and context of the case.

(6) Any administrative penalty imposed by the Board which exceeds one thousand five hundred euro (€1,500) shall be subject to publication on the Board’s website in accordance with policies and procedures established from time to time by the Board:

Provided that the above-mentioned threshold shall not apply in the case of payment service providers and crypto-assets service providers, where the Board may, if considered necessary and proportionate, after a case-by-case evaluation, publish administrative penalties and directives imposed in accordance with its policies and procedures.

## 36. Administrative penalties for breaches of article 33.

(1) Any legal person who breaches the provisions of article 33, shall be liable to an administrative penalty of up to ten percent (10%) of its total annual net turnover in the preceding business year.

(2) Where the legal person is a subsidiary of parent undertaking, or of any undertaking which effectively exercises a dominant influence over that legal person, the relevant turnover shall be the turnover resulting from the consolidated accounts of the ultimate parent undertaking in the preceding business year.

## 37. Appeals from administrative penalties.

(1) Any person, entity or body who feels aggrieved by an administrative penalty imposed by the Board in accordance with articles 14, 35 and 36, whether imposed in respect of one or more breaches, may appeal such administrative penalty both on points of law and fact.

*   Cap. 12.*

(2) The appeal referred to in sub-article (1) shall lie before the Court of Appeal (Inferior Jurisdiction) constituted in terms of article 41(9) of the Code of Organization and Civil Procedure.

*         Cap. 12.*

(3) An appeal before the Court of Appeal (Inferior Jurisdiction) in terms of sub-article (1) shall be filed in the registry of the said Court within twenty (20) working days from the date when the imposition of the administrative penalty is served to the person, entity or body and, saving the provisions of this article, such an appeal shall be regulated by the applicable provisions in accordance with Title VI of Part I of Book Second of the Code of Organization and Civil Procedure relating to appeals.

(4) The Court of Appeal (Inferior Jurisdiction) shall without delay appoint a date for the hearing of the appeal.

(5) For the purposes of the confidentiality of information and documents relating to the proceedings, the appeal before the Court of Appeal (Inferior Jurisdiction) filed in terms of this article shall be heard in camera.

## 38. Recovery of administrative penalties.           Cap. 12.

(1) An administrative penalty, served on the person, entity or body against whom it is imposed by means of a notice in writing, which is not appealed in accordance with the provisions of article 37 and which is not paid within twenty (20) working days from the date of service, shall be recoverable as a civil debt, and the notice in writing shall, upon the service of a copy thereof by means of a judicial act on the subject person indicated in the notice, constitute an executive title for all effects and purposes of Title VII of Part I of Book Second of the Code of Organization and Civil Procedure.

(2) Where the administrative penalty is appealed and is subsequently confirmed in whole or in part, such administrative penalty shall be enforceable in accordance with the procedure provided for in sub-article (1).

## 39. Transitory provisions.  Act No. XXXV of 2025. Cap. 365.

Any criminal proceedings that were commenced prior to the coming into force of this Act shall continue to be regulated by the provisions of the National Interest (Enabling Powers) Act as in force prior to the coming into force of this Act.

## 40. Repeal and saving. Cap. 365.

The National Interest (Enabling Powers) Act is hereby repealed without prejudice to anything done or omitted to be done thereunder, and subject to article 39.
