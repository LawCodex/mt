# Ambient Air Quality Regulations (S.L. 549.59)

**SUBSIDIARY LEGISLATION 549.59**

**AMBIENT AIR QUALITY REGULATIONS**

9th November, 2010 LEGAL NOTICE 478 of 2010, as amended by Legal Notices 482 of 2011, 33 of 2015 and 415 of 2016 and 133 of 2021.

## 1. Citation.

*Amended by: L.N. 415 of 2016; L.N. 133 of 2021.*

(1) The title of these regulations is the Ambient Air Quality Regulations.

(2) These regulations also transpose Directive 2004/107/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 December 2004 relating to arsenic, cadmium, mercury and nickel and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in ambient air; Directive 2008/50/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 21 May 2008 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe; and Commission Directive (EU) 2015/1480 of 28 August 2015 amending several annexes to Directives 2004/107/ EC and 2008/50/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council laying down the rules concerning reference methods, data validation and location of sampling points for the assessment of ambient air quality.

## 2. Interpretation.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015; L.N. 133 of 2021.*

For the purpose of these regulations and unless the context otherwise requires:

*Cap. 549.*

"the Act" means the Environment Protection Act, or any other Act substituting it;

"agglomeration" means a zone that is a conurbation with a population in excess of 250,000 inhabitants or, where the population is 250,000 inhabitants or less, with a given population density per km2 to be established by the competent authority;

"air quality plans" means plans that set out measures in order to attain the limit values or target values;

"alert threshold" means a level beyond which there is a risk to human health from brief exposure for the population as a whole and at which immediate steps are to be taken by the competent authority;

*S.L. 424.15*

"ambient air" means outdoor air in the troposphere, excluding workplaces within the scope of the Work Place (Minimum Health and Safety Requirements) Regulations, where provisions concerning health and safety at work apply and to which members of the public do not have regular access;

"arsenic", "cadmium", "nickel" and "benzo(a)pyrene" mean the total content of these elements and compounds in the PM10 fraction;

"assessment" means any method used to measure, calculate, predict or estimate levels;

"average exposure indicator" means an average level determined on the basis of measurements at urban background locations throughout Malta and which reflects population exposure. It is used to calculate the national exposure reduction target and the exposure concentration obligation;

"the competent authority" means the Environment and Resources Authority, or such other authority as may substitute it;

"contributions from natural sources" means emissions of pollutants not caused directly or indirectly by human activities, including natural events such as volcanic eruptions, seismic activities, geothermal activities, wild-land fires, highwind events, sea sprays or the atmospheric re-suspension or transport of natural particles from dry regions;

"critical level" means a level fixed on the basis of scientific knowledge, above which direct adverse effects may occur on some receptors, such as trees, other plants or natural ecosystems but not on humans;

"exposure concentration obligation" means a level fixed on the basis of the average exposure indicator with the aim of reducing harmful effects on human health, to be attained over a given period;

"emission" means the discharge of substances into the air;

"fixed measurements" means measurements taken at fixed sites, either continuously or by random sampling, to determine the levels in accordance with the relevant data quality objectives;

"group A pollutants" means sulphur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide and oxides of nitrogen, particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5), lead, benzene and carbon monoxide;

"group B pollutants" means Arsenic, Cadmium, Nickel and Benzo(a)pyrene;

"group C pollutants" means benzo(a)anthracene, benzo(b)fluoranthene, benzo(j)fluoranthene, benzo(k) fluoranthene, indeno(1,2,3-cd)pyrene, and dibenzo(a,h) anthracene;

"indicative measurements" means measurements which meet data quality objectives that are less strict than those required for fixed measurements;

"information threshold" means a level beyond which there is a risk to human health from brief exposure for particularly sensitive sections of the population and for which immediate and appropriate information is necessary;

"level" means the concentration of a pollutant in ambient air or the deposition thereof on surfaces in a given time;

"limit value" means a level fixed on the basis of scientific knowledge, with the aim of avoiding, preventing or reducing harmful effects on human health or the environment as a whole, to be attained within a given period and not to be exceeded once attained;

"long-term objective" means a level to be attained in the long term, save where not achievable through proportionate measures, with the aim of providing effective protection of human health and the environment;

"lower assessment threshold" means a level below which modelling or objective-estimation techniques alone may be used to assess ambient air quality;

"margin of tolerance" means the percentage of the limit value by which that value may be exceeded subject to the conditions laid down in these regulations;

"Member State" means a Member State of the European Union;

"the Minister" means the Minister responsible for the environment;

"national exposure reduction target" means a percentage reduction of the average exposure of the population of Malta set for the reference year with the aim of reducing harmful effects on human health, to be attained where possible over a given period;

"operator" means any natural or legal person who operates any industrial concern which has an adverse impact on air quality, or who has or has been delegated decisive economic power over the concern;

"oxides of nitrogen" means the sum of the volume mixing ratio (ppbv) of nitrogen monoxide (nitric oxide) and nitrogen dioxide expressed in units of mass concentration of nitrogen dioxide (μg/ m3);

"ozone precursor substances" means substances which contribute to the formation of ground-level ozone, some of which are listed in Schedule 6;

"PM10" means particulate matter which passes through a size-selective inlet as defined in the reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM10, EN 12341, with a 50% efficiency cut-off at 10 μm aerodynamic diameter;

"PM2.5" means particulate matter which passes through a size-selective inlet as defined in the reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM2.5, EN 14907, with a 50% efficiency cut-off at 2.5 μm aerodynamic diameter;

"pollutant" means any substance present in ambient air and likely to have harmful effects on human health or the environment as a whole;

"polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons" means those organic compounds, composed of at least two fused aromatic rings made entirely from carbon and hydrogen;

"target value" means a level fixed with the aim of avoiding, preventing or reducing harmful effects on human health or the environment as a whole, to be attained where possible over a given period;

"Technical instruction on Air Quality Control" means the latest guidance note approved by the competent authority on air quality control;

"total gaseous mercury" means elemental mercury vapour (Hg0) and reactive gaseous mercury, such as water-soluble mercury species with sufficiently high vapour pressure to exist in the gas phase;

"total or bulk deposition" means the total mass of pollutants which is transferred from the atmosphere to surfaces (such as soil, vegetation, water, buildings, etc.) in a given area within a given time;

"upper assessment threshold" means a level below which a combination of fixed measurements and modelling techniques or indicative measurements may be used to assess ambient air quality;

"urban background locations" means places in urban areas where levels are representative of the exposure of the general urban population;

"volatile organic compounds" (VOC) means organic compounds from anthropogenic and biogenic sources, other than methane, that are capable of producing photochemical oxidants by reactions with nitrogen oxides in the presence of sunlight;

"zone" means any part of Malta as delimited by the competent authority for the purposes of air quality assessment and management.

## 3. Implementations and responsibilities.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) The competent authority shall be responsible:

(a) for the assessment of ambient air quality;

(b) for ensuring the accuracy of measurements;

(c) for the analysis of assessment methods;

(d) for the coordination of Community-wide quality assurance programmes organised by the European Commission;

(e) for cooperating with other Member States and with the European Commission;

(f) for the approval of measurement systems (methods, equipment, networks and laboratories).

(2) The Minister shall designate the competent authorities responsible for the approval of measurement systems (methods, equipment, networks and laboratories).

## 4. Nature of measures.

Measures taken under regulations 33(3) and 37 shall take into account an integrated approach to the protection of air, water and soil and shall not have a negative effect on any other Member State.

## 5. Malta to be divided into zones and agglomerations.

(1) The competent authority shall, for the purposes of these regulations, divide Malta into zones and agglomerations.

(2) The competent authority shall assess and manage air quality throughout all the zones and agglomerations.

(3) The competent authority may establish different zones for different pollutants where this is considered appropriate.

## 6. Assessment thresholds.

Schedule 2 prescribes the following upper and lower assessment thresholds:

(a) for Group A pollutants, the upper and lower assessment thresholds set out in paragraphs 1 to 6 of Section A of the said Schedule; and

(b) for Group B pollutants, the upper and lower assessment thresholds set out in paragraph 7 of Section A of the said Schedule.

## 7. Long term objectives for ozone.

Part III of Section D of Schedule 7 prescribes the long term objectives for ozone.

## 8. Classification of zones.

(1) Zones shall be classified in relation to the assessment thresholds in regulation 6 and the long term objectives in regulation 7.

(2) Classification of the zones under sub-regulation (1) shall be reviewed at least every five years in accordance with the procedure laid down in Section B of Schedule 2 for both group A and group B pollutants.

(3) Classification shall be reviewed earlier in the event of a significant change in the activities relevant to the concentrations of both groups of pollutants in ambient air.

## 9. Competent authority to assess ambient air quality.

The competent authority shall assess ambient air quality with respect to group A and group B pollutants and ozone in all the zones and agglomerations in accordance with the criteria laid down in Schedule 3 and in accordance with the criteria in regulations 10, 11 and 12.

## 10. Competent authority to assess concentrations.

(1) The competent authority shall assess the concentration of group A pollutants and of group B pollutants in those zones and agglomerations in which the levels of these pollutants exceed the upper assessment thresholds in regulation 6.

(2) The competent authority shall assess the concentration of ozone in those zones and agglomerations where the long term objectives specified in regulation 7 have been exceeded during any of the previous five years of measurements, during which fixed measurements were recorded.

(3) In order to comply with the requirements of sub-regulations (1) and (2), the competent authority shall assess the concentrations of:

(a) group A substances by fixed continuous measurements;

(b) group B substances by fixed continuous measurements or by random sampling. The number of measurements shall be sufficient to enable the levels to be determined;

(c) ozone by fixed continuous measurements.

(4) Measurements in sub-regulation (3) may be supplemented by modelling techniques or indicative measurements to provide adequate information on the spatial distribution of the ambient air quality.

## 11. Method of assessing concentrations below the upper threshold.

(1) The competent authority shall assess the concentrations of group A and group B pollutants by a combination of fixed measurements and modelling techniques or indicative measurements in those zones and agglomerations where the concentration of these pollutants is below the upper assessment threshold but above the lower assessment threshold.

(2) In order to determine whether the concentrations of group B pollutants in the respective zones and agglomerations are within the concentration range in sub-regulation (1), the competent authority shall use the method in Section B of Schedule 2.

(3) In order to comply with the requirements of sub-regulation (1) with respect to group B pollutants, the competent authority may use the indicative measurements referred to in Part I of Section B of Schedule 1.

## 12. Method of assuming concentrations below the lower threshold.

(1) The competent authority shall assess the concentrations of group A and group B pollutants by modelling techniques or objective estimation techniques, in those zones and agglomerations were the concentration of these pollutants is below the lower assessment threshold.

(2) In order to determine whether the concentrations of group B pollutants in the respective zones and agglomerations are below the concentration in sub-regulation (1), the competent authority shall use the method in Section B of Schedule 2.

## 13. Benzo(a)pyrene contribution in ambient air.

The competent authority shall assess the contribution of benzo(a)pyrene in ambient air by monitoring also for the group C pollutants at a limited number of measurement sites. The monitoring sites for the group C pollutants shall be co-located with the sampling sites for benzo(a)pyrene and shall be selected in such a way that the long term trends can be identified. Part II of Section B, Part II of Section C and Part I of Section D of Schedule 3 shall apply.

## 14. Measurements to be taken at rural location.

(1) In addition to the requirements of regulations 10, 11 and 12, the competent authority shall measure the concentration of group A pollutants, of group B pollutants, of group C pollutants, mercury and of ozone at a rural background location away from significant sources of air pollution.

(2) The competent authority shall also measure the total deposition of group B pollutants, group C pollutants and mercury at the background site.

(3) The competent authority shall use the readings from the background station in order to assess the total mass concentration and the chemical speciation of PM2.5 on an annual average basis. The data quality objectives in Parts I and III of Section A of Schedule 1 shall apply for the mass concentration measurements of particulate matter. Schedule 4 shall apply in its entirety.

## 15. Sampling points.

(1) Sampling points shall be determined using the criteria listed in the relevant sections of Schedule 3.

(2) The minimum number of sampling points in each zone or agglomeration shall not be less than the minimum number of sampling points prescribed by Schedule 5 as follows:

(a) Part I(1) of Section A of Schedule 5 for group A pollutants;

(b) Section B (1) of Schedule 5 for group B pollutants; and

(c) Part I of Section C of Schedule 5 for ozone.

## 16. Nitrogen dioxide measurements.

(1) The competent authority shall measure the concentration of nitrogen dioxide at a minimum of 50% of the ozone sampling points required under Part I of Section C of Schedule 5. The measurement shall be continuous.

(2) The number of sampling points for the fixed measurement of ozone in those zones and agglomerations where during each of the previous five years of measurement, concentrations were below the long term objectives, shall be in accordance with Part II of Section C of Schedule 5.

(3) The competent authority shall set up at least one sampling point in order to measure the concentrations of the ozone precursor substances listed in Schedule 6, taking into account the objectives and methods laid down in the said schedule.

## 17. Data quality objectives.

The competent authority shall achieve the data quality objectives laid down in Schedule 1.

## 18. Reference methods.

The competent authority shall use the reference methods in Schedule 10 in order to measure the:

(a) concentrations in ambient air of group A pollutants;

(b) concentrations in ambient air of ozone; and

(c) concentrations in ambient air and deposition rates of group B pollutants.

## 19. Action to be taken by the competent authority on group A and group B pollutants.

(1) The competent authority shall take any action it deems necessary in accordance with the powers granted to it under the Act, to make sure that the levels of group A and group B pollutants do not exceed the applicable limit values or target values prescribed by the relevant sections of Schedule 7, in those zones and agglomerations in which the ambient levels of these pollutants are below the applicable limit or target values.

(2) The best ambient air quality, compatible with sustainable development shall be maintained throughout these zones and agglomerations.

## 20. Other action to be taken by the competent authority.

(1) The competent authority shall take any action it deems necessary in accordance with the powers granted to it under the Act in order to ensure that throughout the zones and agglomerations in Malta:

(a) the levels of sulphur dioxide, PM10, lead and carbon monoxide do not exceed the applicable limit values prescribed by the relevant sections of Schedule 7; and (b) the levels of benzene and nitrogen dioxide do not exceed the applicable limit values prescribed by the relevant sections of Schedule 7 from the dates specified therein; and

(c) as from the 31 December 2012 the levels of group B pollutants do not exceed the target values prescribed by the relevant sections of Schedule 7.

(2) Compliance with these requirements shall be assessed in accordance with Schedule 3.

(3) The margins of tolerance laid down in Schedule 7 shall apply in accordance with regulation 31(1).

(4) The alert thresholds for concentrations of sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide in ambient air shall be those prescribed by Section A of Schedule 8.

## 21. Target values for group B pollutants.

The competent authority shall draw up a list of zones and agglomerations where the target values in Schedule 7 for group B pollutants are exceeded. The competent authority shall specify the areas of exceedance and the sources contributing thereto.

## 22. Critical levels for protection of vegetation.

(1) The critical levels for the protection of vegetation shall be those prescribed by Schedule 9 and assessed in accordance with Section A of Schedule 3.

(2) Where fixed measurements are the sole source of information for assessing air quality, the number of sampling points shall not be less than the minimum number specified in Part III of Section A of Schedule 5.

(3) Where information for assessing air quality is supplemented by indicative measurements or modelling, the minimum number of sampling points may be reduced by up to 50% as long as the assessed concentrations of the relevant pollutant can be assessed in accordance with the data quality objectives prescribed by Part I of Section A of Schedule 1.

## 23. National PM2.5 exposure reduction target.

(1) The national PM2.5 exposure reduction target shall be that prescribed by Part II of Section B of Schedule 7. This reduction shall be attained by the year specified therein.

(2) By 2015, the average exposure indicator shall not exceed the exposure concentration obligation prescribed by Part III of Section B of Schedule 7. The PM2.5 average exposure indicator shall be assessed as prescribed by Part I of Section B of Schedule 7.

(3) In accordance with Schedule 3, the distribution and number of sampling points on which the average exposure indicator for PM2.5 is based shall adequately reflect the general population exposure. The number of sampling points shall be no less than that prescribed by Part II of Section A of Schedule 5.

## 24. PM2.5 concentrations shall not exceed target value.

(1) Concentrations of PM2.5 in ambient air shall not exceed the target value prescribed by Part IV of Section A of Schedule 7, by the date specified therein.

(2) Concentrations of PM2.5 in ambient air shall not exceed the limit value prescribed by Part V of Section A of Schedule 7 throughout the zone and the agglomeration, by the date specified therein. Compliance with this requirement shall be assessed as prescribed by Schedule 3.

(3) The margins of tolerance in Part V of Section A of Schedule 7 shall apply in accordance with regulation 32(1).

## 25. Ozone target value.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) The target values for ozone shall be those prescribed in Part II of Section D of Schedule 7.

(2) The long-term objectives for ozone shall be those prescribed in Part III of Section D of Schedule 7.

(3) In those zones and agglomerations in which the levels of ozone in ambient air are higher than the target values in sub-regulation (1), these target values shall be attained as from the dates prescribed in Part II of Section D of Schedule 7, through the implementation of the programme prepared pursuant to the National Emissions Ceilings for Certain Atmospheric Pollutants Regulations and if necessary through the implementation of an air quality plan.

*S.L. 549.32*

(4) In those zones and agglomerations in which the levels of ozone in ambient air are higher than the long-term objectives in sub-regulation (2), but lower than or equal to the target values in sub-regulation (1), the long-term objectives shall be achieved through the implementation of cost-effective measures. These measures shall at least be consistent with all the air quality plans and the programmes in sub-regulation (3).

(5) The levels of ozone in ambient air shall be kept below the long-term objectives in sub-regulation (2) in those zones and agglomerations in which the levels of ozone in ambient air meet these long-term objectives, taking into consideration the transboundary nature of ozone pollution and meteorological conditions. The best ambient air quality compatible with a high level of environmental and human health protection shall be preserved.

Any air quality studies required under any other regulations issued under the Act shall be carried out according to the guidance document issued under regulation 26(2) and shall make reference to the pollutant levels in regulations 19 and 20.

## 30. Air Quality Plan sub-committee.

(1) An Air Quality Plan sub-committee is hereby being appointed with an aim of being consulted by the competent authority prior to the issuing of the Action Plans under regulation 33(1).

(2) The composition of the sub-committee shall consist of:

(a) the Director of Environmental Health or his representative;

(b) the Director of Environment Protection (Environment and Resources Authority) or his representative;

(c) the Director of Planning (Planning Authority) or his representative;

(d) the Chief Executive Officer of the Transport Authority or his representative;

(e) the representative of the Malta Chamber of Commerce, Enterprise and Industry;

(f) the representative of the Malta Chamber of Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs);

(g) the representative of the Local Councils Association;

(h) a representative of the Environmental NGOs;

(i) a representative of the Public Health NGOs.

## 31. Air quality report.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015; L.N. 133 of 2021.*

(1) In order to assess compliance with the limit values, target values, long-term objectives and the national exposure reduction target in Schedule 7 the competent authority shall compile an air quality report on a yearly basis. The report shall be due on the 30th November of each subsequent year.

(2) The report shall include an analysis of the results of the monitoring of the atmospheric levels of group A and group B pollutants and ozone throughout Malta and shall also summarise the levels exceeding limit values, target values, long-term objectives, information thresholds and alert thresholds, for the relevant averaging periods. This information shall be combined with a summary assessment of the effects of these exceedances.

(3) These reports may also include, where appropriate, further information such as information on other pollutants such as the selected non-regulated ozone precursor substances as listed in part B of Schedule 6.

## 32. Air Quality Management Area.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) The Minister shall, upon the competent authority’s advice, declare an Air Quality Management Area wherever:

(a) the limit values, target values, long term objectives or the national exposure reduction target in Schedule 7, are not being achieved, keeping in mind the dates by when the limit values, target values, long term objectives or the national exposure reduction target are to be achieved;

(b) it is forecasted, through the use of predictive modelling software that the limit values, target values, long term objectives or the national exposure reduction target in Schedule 7, will be exceeded.

(2) The Air Quality Management area shall be declared by the issuing of an Air Quality Management Order in terms of the details in Schedule 13. The Air Quality Management Order shall be published in the Gazette.

(3) An order made under sub-regulation (2) may be revoked if as a result of a subsequent air quality assessment it is shown that the limit values, target values, long term objectives and the national exposure reduction target in Schedule 7 are being achieved in a location which had been previously designated as an Air Quality Management Area under sub-regulation (1).

## 33. Air Quality Action Plan.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) Where an order under regulation 32(2) comes into effect, the competent authority shall, before the expiration of the twelve month period beginning with the publication of the order in the Gazette, prepare an Air Quality Action Plan for the area in question, which shall aim to keep the exceedance period as short as possible.

(2) The Action Plan referred to in sub-regulation (1) shall, as a minimum, include:

(a) the information in Schedule 11; and

(b) a statement of any time within which the measures comprised in the action plan are to be implemented. (3) The Action Plans compiled under sub-regulation (1) shall also include all those measures necessary in order to ensure that the limit values, target values, long term objectives or the national exposure target in Schedule 7 will be complied within the area in question. Action plans shall contain measures which can, through the use of predictive modelling software amongst other techniques, be shown to cause a decrease in the ambient levels of the pollutants concerned in the area in question, and may include amongst other things:

(a) the restriction or rerouting of traffic flows;

(b) temporary bans on the passage of heavy goods vehicles not compliant with certain Euro Standards (to be determined by modelling);

(c) temporary suspension of any commercial or industrial activity having an adverse effect on air quality, such as stone quarrying, stone cutting and polishing, and industrial plants, among others;

(d) measures in relation to ships at berth in nearby ports;

(e) time-limits on the granting of development planning permits; and

(f) suspension of any construction works in the area.

(4) In the compilation of the action plan, the competent authority shall consult the sub-committee appointed under regulation 30.

(5) If the air quality action plan in sub-regulation (1) is triggered by an exceedance of the environmental quality objectives in sub-regulation (3) for more than 1 pollutant then the competent authority shall prepare and implement an integrated air quality action plan targeting all the pollutants concerned.

## 34. Revision of Action Plan.

The competent authority may from time to time revise the action plan issued under regulation 33(1).

## 35. Consistency of plans.

*Amended by: L.N. 133 of 2021.*

Any action plan issued under regulation 33(1), shall be consistent with any plans required under the -

*S.L. 549.56*

(a) Large Combustion Plants Regulations;

*S.L. 549.124*

(b) Limitation of Emissions of Certain Atmospheric Pollutants Regulations; and

*S.L. 549.37*

(c) Assessment and Management of Environment Noise Regulations,

in order to achieve the relevant environmental objectives.

## 36. Short Term Air Quality Action plans.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) The competent authority shall draw up Short Term Air Quality Action plans for those zones and agglomerations for which there is as risk that the pollutant levels exceed one or more of the alert thresholds in Schedule 8.

(2) The Short Term Air Quality Action plan shall take into account Decision 2004/279/EC and shall indicate the measures to be taken in order to reduce the risk or duration of the exceedances in sub-regulation (1).

## 37. Contents of action plans.

The action plans compiled under regulation 33(1) shall include all those measures necessary in order to ensure that the alert thresholds in Schedule 8 will be not be exceeded in the zones and agglomerations in question. These action plans shall contain measures which can through the use of predictive modelling software, amongst other techniques, be shown to cause a decrease in the ambient levels of the pollutants concerned in the area in question, and may include amongst other things:

(a) the restriction or rerouting of traffic flows;

(b) temporary bans on the passage of heavy goods vehicles not compliant with certain Euro Standards (to be determined by modelling);

(c) temporary suspension of any commercial or industrial activity having an adverse effect on air quality are carried out, such as stone quarrying, stone cutting and polishing, and industrial plants, among others;

(d) measures in relation to ships at berth in nearby ports;

(e) time-limits on the granting of development planning permits; and

(f) suspension of any construction works in the area.

## 38. Ozone exceedances.

In case of exceedances of the alert thresholds for ozone as prescribed by Schedule 8, action plans in regulation 33(1) will only be required if following an assessment carried out by the competent authority, it results that taking the national, geographical, meteorological and economic consideration into account the action plan will result in a reduction of the risk, duration or severity of such an exceedance.

## 39. Information to be imparted by media.

The general public shall be duly and effectively informed by means of radio, television, newspapers and the internet amongst other things, of any exceedances of the information and alert thresholds prescribed by Schedule 8.

## 40. Further information to be given to the public.

*Amended by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) The competent authority shall adequately and in good time inform the general public as well as appropriate organisations, including environmental organisations, consumer organisations, organisations representing the interests of sensitive populations, other relevant health care bodies and the relevant industrial federations, of the following:

(a) ambient air quality in accordance with Schedule 12;

(b) any postponement decisions taken pursuant to Article 22(1) of Directive 2008/50/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of the 21 May 2008 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe;

(c) any exemptions pursuant to Article 22(2) of Directive 2008/50/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of the 21 May 2008 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe;

(d) air quality plans as provided for in Articles 22(1), 23 and the programmes referred to in Article 17(2) of Directive 2008/50/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of the 21 May 2008 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe and regulations 33(1) and 36(1) as well as the programme in regulation 25(3).

(2) The competent authority shall make the information in sub-regulation (1) available free of charge by means of any accessible media including the Internet or any other appropriate means of telecommunication, and shall take into account the provisions laid down in Directive 2007/2/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 March 2007 establishing an Infrastructure for Spatial Information in the European Community (INSPIRE).

(3) The competent authority shall make available to the public the annual reports for all pollutants required by regulation 31(1).

(4) The competent authority shall also inform the public of the allocation of responsibilities in terms of the provisions of regulation 3.

## 40A. Competent authority to initiate process.

*Added by: L.N. 33 of 2015.*

(1) Where any alert threshold, limit value, target value or long-term objective is exceeded due to significant transboundary transport of air pollutants or their precursors from neighbouring EU Member States, the competent authority shall contact the competent authorities within the concerned Member States in order to initiate the process in Article 25(1) of Directive 2008/50/EC, the aim of which is to cooperate and, where appropriate, draw up joint activities, such as the preparation of joint or coordinated air quality plans pursuant to Article 23 of Directive 2008/50/EC in order to remove such exceedances through the application of appropriate but proportionate measures.

(2) The competent authority shall, if appropriate, pursuant to Article 24 of the Directive approach the competent authorities within the neighbouring Member States in order to prepare and contribute to the implementation of joint short-term action plans covering neighbouring zones in other Member States. The competent authority shall ensure that neighbouring zones in other Member States which have developed short-term action plans receive all appropriate information.

(3) Where the information threshold or alert thresholds are exceeded in zones or agglomerations close to national borders, information shall be provided as soon as possible to the competent authorities in the neighbouring Member States concerned. That information shall also be made available to the public.

(4) In drawing up plans as provided for in sub-regulations (1) and (2) and in informing the public as referred to in sub-regulation (3), the competent authority shall, where appropriate, endeavour to pursue cooperation with third countries, and in particular with candidate countries.

## 41. Offences.

Any person shall be guilty of an offence under these regulations if:

(a) he fails to comply with any order lawfully given in terms of any provision of these regulations; or

(b) he contravenes any restriction, prohibition or requirement imposed by or under these regulations; or (c) he conspires, attempts, aids, or abets, any other person by whatever means, including advertising, counselling or procurement to contravene the provisions of these regulations or to fail to comply with any such provisions, including any order lawfully given in terms of any of the provision of these regulations, or to contravene any restriction, prohibition or requirement imposed by or under the said regulations.

## 42. Penalties.

Any person who commits an offence against these regulations shall, on conviction, be liable:

(a) on a first conviction to a fine (multa) of not less than one thousand two hundred euro (€1,200) but not exceeding two thousand three hundred euro (€2,300);

(b) on a second or subsequent conviction, to a fine (multa) of not less than two thousand three hundred euro (€2,300), but not exceeding eleven thousand six hundred euro (€11,600) or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years, or to both such fine and imprisonment:

(g) Account shall be taken of the need to locate sampling points on islands where it is necessary for the protection of human health.

A. Reference measurement methods

\1. Reference method for the sampling and analysis of arsenic, cadmium and nickel in ambient air.

The reference method for the measurement of arsenic, cadmium and nickel concentrations in ambient air is described in EN 12341:2014. The reference method for the measurement of arsenic, cadmium and nickel in ambient air is that described in EN 14902:2005 “Ambient air quality - Standard method for the measurement of Pb, Cd, As, and Ni in the PM10 fraction of suspended particulate matter”.

\2. Reference method for the sampling of benzene

The reference method for the measurement of benzene is that described in EN 14662:2005, parts 1, 2 and 3 ‘Ambient air quality - Standard method for measurement of benzene concentrations’.

\3. Reference method for the measurement of carbon monoxide

The reference method for the measurement of carbon monoxide is that described in EN 14626:2012 ‘Ambient Air - Standard method for the measurement of the concentration of carbon monoxide by non-dispersive infrared spectroscopy’.

\4. Reference method for the measurement of lead

The reference method for the sampling of lead is that described in section A(8) of this schedule. The reference method for the measurement of lead is that described in EN 14902:2005 ‘Standard method for measurement of Pb/Cd/As/Ni in the PM10 fraction of suspended matter’.

\5. Reference method for the sampling and analysis of mercury

The reference method for the measurement of total gaseous mercury concentrations is that described in EN 15852:2010 “Ambient air quality - Standard method for the determination of total gaseous mercury”.

\6. Reference method for the measurement of nitrogen dioxide and oxides of nitrogen

The reference method for the measurement of nitrogen dioxide and oxides of nitrogen is that described in EN 14211:2012 ‘Ambient Air - Standard method for the measurement of the concentration of nitrogen dioxide and nitrogen monoxide by chemiluminescence’.

\7. Reference method for measurement of ozone

The reference method for the measurement of ozone is that described in EN 14625:2012 ‘Ambient Air - Standard method for the measurement of the concentration of ozone by ultraviolet photometry’.

\8. Reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM10

The reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM10 is that described in EN 12341:2014 ‘Ambient Air - standard gravimetric measurement for the determination of the PM10 or PM2,5 mass concentration of suspended particulate matter’.

\9. Reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM2.5

The reference method for the sampling and measurement of PM10 is that described in EN 12341:2014 ‘Ambient Air – standard gravimetric measurement for the determination of the PM10 or PM2,5 mass concentration of suspended particulate matter’.

\10. Reference method for the sampling and analysis of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons

The reference method for the measurement of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in ambient air is described EN 12341:2014. The reference method for the measurement of benzo(a)pyrene in ambient air is that described in EN 15549:2008 “Air quality - Standard method for the measurement of concentration of benzo[a]pyrene in ambient air”. In the absence of a CEN standard method, for the other polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (group C pollutants excluding benzo[a]pyrene), an ISO standard method such as ISO 12884 or any national standard method from any of the EU Member States can be used.

\11. Reference method for the measurement of sulphur dioxide

The reference method for the measurement of sulphur dioxide is that described in EN 14212:2012 ‘Ambient Air - Standard method for the measurement of the concentration of sulphur dioxide by fluorescence’.

\12. Reference method for the sampling and analysis of the deposition rates of group B pollutants

The reference method for the determination of the deposition of arsenic, cadmium and nickel is that described in EN 15841:2009 “Ambient air quality - Standard method for the determination of arsenic, cadmium, lead and nickel in atmospheric deposition”.

The reference method for the determination of the deposition of mercury is that described in EN 15853:2010 “Ambient air quality - Standard method for determination of mercury deposition”.

The reference method for the determination of the deposition of benzo[a]pyrene and the other polycyclic hydrocarbons referred to in regulation 13 is that described in EN 15980:2011 “Air Quality. Determination of the deposition of benz[a]anthracene, benzo[b]fluoranthene, benzo[j]fluoranthene, benzo[k]fluoranthene, benzo[a]pyrene, dibenzo[a,h]anthracene and indeno[1,2,3-cd]pyrene”.

B. Demonstration of equivalence

It is possible to use any other method which be shown to give results which are equivalent to any of the methods referred to in Section A, or in the case of particulate matter, any other method which displays a consistent relationship to the reference method. In that event results achieved by that method must be corrected to produce results equivalent to those that would have been achieved by using the reference method.

\2. Whenever possible the correction factor should also be applied retroactively to past measurement data in order to achieve better data comparability.

C. Standardisation

For gaseous pollutants the volume must be standardised at a temperature of 293 K and an atmospheric pressure of 101.3 kPa. For particulate matter and substances to be analysed in particulate matter (e.g. lead) the sampling volume refers to ambient conditions in terms of temperature and atmospheric pressure at the date of measurements.

D. Mutual recognition of data

When demonstrating that the equipment meets the performance requirements of the reference methods listed in Section A of this Schedule, the competent authority pursuant to regulation 3 shall accept test reports issued in other European Union Member States provided that the test laboratories are accredited to the relevant harmonised standard for testing and calibration laboratories.

The detailed test reports and all the results of the tests shall be available to other competent authorities or their designated bodies. Test reports shall demonstrate that the equipment meets all the performance requirements including where some environmental and site conditions are specific to a EU Member State and are outside the conditions for which the equipment has been already tested and type approved in another EU Member State.

**Public Information**

\1. Up-to-date information on ambient concentrations of the pollutants covered by these regulations shall be routinely made available to the public.

\2. Ambient concentrations provided shall be presented as average values according to the appropriate averaging period as laid down in Schedule 7. The information shall at least indicate any levels exceeding air quality objectives including limit values, target values, alert thresholds, information thresholds or long term objectives of the regulated pollutant. It shall also provide a short assessment in relation to the air quality objectives of the regulated pollutant. It shall also provide a short assessment in relation to the air quality objectives and appropriate information regarding effects on health, or, where appropriate, vegetation.

\3. Information on ambient concentrations of sulphur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide, particulate matter (at least PM10), ozone and carbon monoxide shall be updated on at least a daily basis, and, wherever practicable, information shall be updated on an hourly basis. Information on ambient concentrations of lead and benzene, presented as an average value for the last 12 months, shall be updated on a three-monthly basis, and on a monthly basis, wherever practicable.

\4. Timely information about actual or predicted exceedances of alert thresholds and any information threshold is provided to the public. The details supplied shall include at least the following information:

(a) Information on observed exceedance(s):

\- Location or area of the exceedance,

\- Type of threshold exceeded (information or alert),

\- Start time and duration of the exceedance,

\- Highest one hour concentration and in addition highest eight hour mean concentration in the case of ozone:

(b) Forecast for the following afternoon/day(s):

\- Geographical area of expected exceedances of information and/or alert threshold,

\- Expected changes in pollution (improvement, stabilisation or deterioration), together with reasons for those changes;

(c) Information on the type of population concerned, possible health effects and recommended behaviour:

\- Information on population groups at risk,

\- Description of likely symptoms,

\- Recommended precautions to be taken by the population concerned,

\- Where to find further information;

(d) Information on preventive action to reduce pollution and/or exposure to it: indication of main source sectors; recommendations for action to reduce emissions;

(e) In case of predicted exceedances, steps shall be taken to ensure that such details are supplied to the extent practicable.
