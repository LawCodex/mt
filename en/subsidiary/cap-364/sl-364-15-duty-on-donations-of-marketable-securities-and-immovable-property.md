# Duty on Donations of Marketable Securities and Immovable Property Used for Business (Exemption) Order (S.L. 364.15)

**SUBSIDIARY LEGISLATION 364.15**

**DUTY ON DONATIONS OF MARKETABLE**

SECURITIES AND IMMOVABLE PROPERTY USED

**FOR BUSINESS (EXEMPTION) ORDER**

2nd May, 2016

LEGAL NOTICE 131 of 2017, as amended by Legal Notices 82 of 2018 and 320 and 412 of 2018, 281 of 2019, 427 of 2020, 460 of 2021, 366 of 2022, 289 of 2023, 325 of 2024, 283 of 2025 and 250 of 2026.

## 1. Citation.

The title of this order is the Duty on Donations of Marketable Securities and Immovable Property Used for Business (Exemption) Order.

## 2. Marketable securities. Cap. 364              Cap. 123.

(1) Notwithstanding any other provision of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act:

(a) where an individual owns marketable securities issued by a company, the transfer of which marketable securities would be chargeable to duty, duty shall be chargeable at a rate (referred to hereinafter as "the reduced rate") of one euro and fifty cents (€1.50) on every one hundred euro (€100.00) or part thereof of the real value of those marketable securities where those marketable securities are transferred by gratuitous title by the individual to persons referred to in article 5(2)(e)(i) of the Income Tax Act:

*   Cap. 16   Cap. 565    Cap. 364     Cap. 123*

(b) where an individual transfers by gratuitous title immovable property being a commercial tenement as defined in article 1525 of the Civil Code (hereinafter referred to as "business property"), that had been used in a family business as defined in the Family Business Act for a period of at least three (3) years preceding the transfer, in assessing the duty chargeable in terms of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act, if any such duty is chargeable, it shall be charged at the reduced rate where that business property is transferred by gratuitous title by the individual to persons referred to in article 5(2)(e)(i) of the Income Tax Act.

(2) When the reduced rate is applied on the transfer by gratuitous title of marketable securities or business property referred to in this article, no other exemption or relief from duty shall apply to the said transfer.

## 3. Applicability.

*Amended by: L.N.82 of 2018; L.N. 320 of 2018; L.N. 412 of 2018; L.N. 281 of 2019; L.N. 427 of 2020; L.N. 460 of 2021; L.N. 366 of 2022; L.N. 289 of 2023; L.N. 325 of 2024; L.N. 283 of 2025.*

This order applies to a transfer by gratuitous title that is made on or after the 1st April 2017 but prior to 1st January 2027.

## 4. Transfer by gratuitous title.

*Amended by: L.N.82 of 2018; L.N. 320 of 2018; L.N. 412 of 2018; L.N. 281 of 2019; L.N. 427 of 2020; L.N. 460 of 2021; L.N. 366 of 2022; L.N. 289 of 2023; L.N. 325 of 2024; L.N. 283 of 2025.*

(1) Any transfer by gratuitous title claiming benefits in terms of this order shall be made by public deed:

*    Cap. 364. S.L. 364.06.*

Provided that benefits in terms of this order shall only be granted in respect of a transfer by gratuitous title where the notice required in terms of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act and the Duty on Documents and Transfers Rules has been submitted to the Commissioner for Revenue on the form or other means provided by the Commissioner on or before the 31st December 2026.

*        Cap. 565*

(2) In the case of a transfer falling within the scope of paragraph (b) of article 2, the individual transferring the business property shall provide to the notary who receives the deed of such transfer a report prepared by a certified public accountant holding a practicing certificate in auditing giving assurance that the business property being transferred by gratuitous title has been used in a family business as defined in the Family Business Act for a period of at least three (3) years preceding the transfer.

(3) The Commissioner for Revenue may also request such further information, forms and documentation as he may deem fit, by means of a notice in writing within the period, not being less than thirty (30) days, specified in the said notice.

## 5. Relief.

The relief granted under this order shall be forfeited and the provisions of article 6 shall apply if the individual to whom the transfer by gratuitous title was made, transfers inter vivos the marketable securities or the business property, as the case may be, within a period of three (3) years immediately following the date of the transfer by gratuitous title:

Provided that in the case of a business property, the relief granted under this order shall also be forfeited and the provisions of article 6 shall apply if the business property is not used within a business carried on by the said individual, for a period of three (3) years immediately following the date of the transfer by gratuitous title.

## 6. Relief from duty.      Cap. 364.

When the relief from duty under this order is forfeited as provided in article 5, the provisions of article 2 shall not apply and the duty chargeable on the transfer referred to in that article shall be the duty that would have been chargeable in accordance with the provisions of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act had the relief under this order not been claimed and availed of.

## 7. Duty on a transfer.

(1) In the event that duty on a transfer becomes chargeable in accordance with article 6, the excess, if any, of the amount of the duty so chargeable over the amount of duty that was paid on the deed of that transfer shall become payable to the Commissioner by the person to whom the transfer was made within a period of thirty (30) days after the date on which the event resulting in the forfeiture of the relief granted under this order takes place:

*           Cap. 565*

Provided that where the duty referred to in this article is payable in respect of business property or part thereof that had previously been transferred by gratuitous title to the particular individual or individuals, and the Commissioner issues a certificate attesting that the said business property that has been transferred inter vivos by the said individual or individuals, is replaced within one (1) year by an immovable property used solely for a similar purpose in the family business as defined in the Family Business Act (hereinafter referred to as the "replacement property"), in assessing the duty chargeable in respect of the replacement property, duty payable in accordance with this article shall be allowed as a deduction against duty chargeable on the acquisition of the replacement property:

Provided further that the deduction allowed in terms of the preceding proviso shall in no case exceed the amount of duty otherwise chargeable, but for the provisions of this article, on the acquisition of the replacement property.

(2) The provisions of rules 5 and 6 shall apply to the replacement property in the same manner as they apply to the business property and references to "business property" in the said rules shall, where applicable, be construed as referring to the replacement property.

## 8. Benefit applicable to certain transfers of business causa mortis.

*Added by: L.N. 250 of 2026. Cap. 364.*

(1) Notwithstanding any other provision of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act, where:

*     Cap. 123.*

(a) proof in writing is produced that demonstrates that prior to his death, an individual, hereinafter referred to as "the deceased", had a genuine intention to make a transfer by gratuitous title to persons referred to in article 5(2)(e)(i) of the Income Tax Act:

Provided that where such proof in writing does not constitute a document referred to in sub-article (3)(b), such proof shall be endorsed by a person referred to in sub-article (3)(d) within a period not exceeding three hundred and sixty-five days (365) from the day the deceased died;

(b) had such transfer been completed immediately before the death of the deceased, the transfer would have satisfied all the conditions and eligibility requirements prescribed in this order;

(c) such transfer was not completed solely by reason of the death of the deceased occurring before the execution of the relevant public deed;

(d) upon the death of the deceased, the property which was intended to be transferred is acquired causa mortis by the said person who had been identified in the proof in writing referred to in paragraph (a) as the intended donee of the proposed transfer; and

(e) such acquisition is not prevented, altered, redirected or otherwise affected by the provisions of any will or other testamentary disposition, or by the operation of any law,

the acquisition causa mortis shall be chargeable with duty at the rate of one euro and fifty cents (€1.50) for every one hundred euro (€100), or part thereof, of the value thereof:

Provided that the provisions of articles 5 and 6 shall also apply in relation to such acquisitions.

(2) The provisions of this article shall only apply where:

(a) the deceased died on a date after 31st December 2025;

(b) the proposed transfer by gratuitous title was bona fide and not for the purpose of avoiding any applicable legal provision;

(c) the proposed transfer had not been abandoned, revoked, withdrawn or materially altered before the death of the deceased; and

(d) the proposed transfer would, but for the death of the deceased, have been completed in the ordinary course of events.

(3) For the purposes of this article, the requirements of sub-articles (1)(a) and (2) may be established by any of the following, as applicable in the circumstances:

(a) any documentation, declaration, notice, certification, confirmation or other evidence required or contemplated under this order;

(b) any document signed by the deceased that constitutes a written promise of donation, agreement, declaration, succession plan, shareholders’ agreement, board resolution or other written instrument evidencing the proposed transfer;

(c) proof that substantial and demonstrable steps had been taken towards completing the proposed transfer;

(d) a declaration made under oath by a warranted advocate, certified public accountant, notary public or other professional person acceptable to the Commissioner, confirming the facts and circumstances giving rise to the claim:

Provided that where the documents referred to in paragraphs (a) and (c) are produced, such shall be accompanied by the declaration referred to in paragraph (d), which declaration shall refer to the documents referred to in paragraphs (a) and (c), as the case may be:

Provided further that the Commissioner may, on the basis of objective evidence, refuse to accept a document referred to in this sub-article where he is not satisfied that such document is appropriate for establishing the requirements of sub-articles (1)(a) and (2).

*         Cap. 364.*

(4) The burden of proving entitlement to the benefit provided for in this article shall rest upon the person identified as the intended donee in the documentary evidence referred to in sub-article (3) who claims the rate referred to in sub-article (1). Where the Commissioner is satisfied of such entitlement, he shall issue a certificate confirming entitlement to the benefit, which certificate shall be attached to the declaration of the relevant transfer causa mortis drawn up in accordance with article 33 of the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act. The Commissioner may determine the forms, procedures, details and documents required for the issue of such certificate.

(5) This article shall only apply where the property devolves upon and is acquired causa mortis by the said person who was identified as the intended donee in the documentary evidence referred to in sub-article (3).

(6) The Commissioner may refuse a claim under this article where he is not satisfied, on the basis of objective evidence, that the intended transfer would have qualified for the reduced rate prescribed under this order had it been completed prior to the death of the deceased.
