## 1. Citation and interpretation.

(1) The title of these bye-laws is the Bye-Laws for the Degree of Doctor of Philosophy - Ph.D. - under the auspices of the Institute of Digital Games.

(2) In these bye-laws, unless the context otherwise requires - "the Board" means the Board of the Institute of Digital Games;

"the Institute" means the Institute of Digital Games;

"the Degree" means the Degree of Doctor of Philosophy - Ph.D.; and

*S.L. 327.101*

"the Principal Regulations" means the Doctor of Philosophy - Ph.D. - Degree Regulations.

## 2. Applicability.

These bye-laws shall be applicable from 1 October 2013 onwards.

## 3. Requirements for admission.

(1) Applicants for the Degree offered by the Institute shall be in possession of one of the following degrees: a Master’s degree, or a Bachelor Honours degree obtained with a First Class classification, in an area of study which is deemed as appropriate by the Board. Key areas include: game analysis, game design and game technology.

(2) The Board may also consider applications from applicants with qualifications at Master’s level or higher in another discipline and who possess experience or knowledge which, in the opinion of the Board, will enable them to pursue doctoral studies successfully.

(3) When the Board deems it necessary, it may require students to successfully complete a number of study-units during the course of their study, under those conditions that it may impose, after approval by Senate.

## 4. Application form.

(1) Applicants may submit their application form, together with a detailed Curriculum Vitae including 3 reference letters, transcripts of qualifications, and a comprehensive research proposal outlining the proposed project, at any time of the year.

(2) Applicants may also be asked to demonstrate competence in skills required by the nature of the proposed research.

## 6. Place of study.

Students are required to spend a total of at least two months per semester at the University of Malta campus, provided that students may be allowed by the Board to spend a maximum of 6 months abroad if this is required by the nature of their research.

## 7. Supervision and students’ progress.

Students shall only be allowed to proceed with their studies subject to satisfactory progress reports from their Principal Supervisor. If the reports are negative, the Board shall make recommendations to Senate on the student’s continuing registration for the Degree.

## 8. Panel of Supervision.

(1) The Board shall appoint a Panel of Supervision for each student, normally composed of three members as follows: the Director as chairman, and two other members, one of whom being the Principal Supervisor, provided that when the Director is the Principal Supervisor, he shall not be appointed Chairman.

(2) The Panel of Supervision shall follow, as it deems fit, the progress of students through the course, provided that it meets at least once every six months to review such progress.

(3) Students shall be required to report to their Principal Supervisor at least once every two months and, in addition, as often as the Principal Supervisor deems necessary.

(4) Principal supervisors shall submit to the Panel of Supervision progress reports every June for each student under their supervision. Such reports may include recommendations to either (a) modify the title and/or content of the thesis; (b) extend the period of study in order to enable the student to complete the thesis; or (c) terminate studies prematurely if the Principal Supervisor deems this to be proper in the circumstances, provided that:

(i) the extension of the study period referred to in (b) shall be in accordance with the provisions of the Principal Regulations; and

(ii) the Board may not terminate studies prematurely according to (c) until it has first given the student a chance to be heard.

## 9. Report on research work.

(1) After twelve months and not later than twenty-four months of full-time study, students are required to submit a report on their research work for consideration by an ad hoc board appointed by the Board. The ad hoc board shall be composed of a chairman, who shall be the Director of the Institute or his delegate, and two examiners, one of whom shall be the Principal Supervisor and the other a member of the Institute as long as the Director is not the Supervisor. In that case, another member shall act as a Chairman.

## 10. Institute’s research and teaching programmes.

Students registered at the Institute may be required to participate in the Institute’s research and teaching programmes.

## 11. Thesis presentation.

Six months before the submission deadline, students shall be informed that they are required to inform the Institute that they will be submitting their thesis for examination not less than three months before the submission deadline.

## 12. Board of Examiners.

The Board shall recommend to Senate the appointment of a Board of Examiners in accordance with the Principal Regulations.

## 13. Format.

The Board shall approve and publish guidelines on the format and presentation of the thesis for the Degree.

## 14. Number of copies.

(1) Students shall be required to submit a soft-bound copy of their thesis for each member of the Board of Examiners, together with a copy in digital format. The officer in charge of the Institute shall issue a dated receipt.

(2) On successful completion of their studies, students shall be required to submit to the Institute three hard-bound copies of the thesis and one copy in digital format within six weeks from the publication of their result. One copy of the thesis shall be kept in the Library of the Institute and another at the University Library. Students who do not comply with this bye-law shall not be awarded the Degree.
