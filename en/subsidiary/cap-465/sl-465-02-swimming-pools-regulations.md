# Swimming Pools Regulations (S.L. 465.02)

**SUBSIDIARY LEGISLATION 465.02**

**SWIMMING POOLS REGULATIONS**

29th April, 2005

LEGAL NOTICE 129 of 2005, as amended by Legal Notice 135 of 2008.

## 1. Citation.

The title of these regulations is the Swimming Pools Regulations.

## 2. Interpretation.

*Amended by: L.N. 135 of 2008. Cap. 465.*

In these regulations, unless the context otherwise requires:

"Act" means the Public Health Act;

"authorised officer" means any duly qualified person authorised by the competent authority to carry out any functions under the Act and includes any nominated officer;

"competent authority" means the Superintendent of Public Health;

"responsible person" means the person responsible for swimming pool and includes, in his absence, such employee or other person, being the senior employee or person present, engaged in operating the swimming pool at the time; such senior employee or person shall be presumed to be acting on the authority of the person responsible unless the person responsible produces proof to the contrary;

"swimming pool" means a public or commercial swimming pool which shall include any artificial basin intended for recreational bathing, swimming, diving, or therapeutic purposes which is located either indoors or outdoors and is provided with controlled water supply and which is not used or intended to be used as a pool at a single family residence unless it is used or intended to be used for commercial or business purposes and shall also include wading pools, spas, whirlpools, hot tubs, diving pools and special purpose pools;

"swimming pools" includes conventional pools used or intended to be used for recreational bathing, swimming and water recreation activities;

"wading pools" includes pools principally used or intended to be used for wading and recreational bathing by small children;

"spas" includes whirlpools, hot tubs and pools used for recreational bathing which are used in conjunction with high velocity air systems, high velocity water circulation systems, hot water, cold water, mineral baths or any combination of these items;

"diving pools" includes pools used only for diving or training and practice of diving techniques; and

"special purpose pools" include pools used exclusively for a particular purpose, including but not limited to water flumes, pools for scuba diving instructions, therapeutic pools, hydrotherapy pools, floatation vessels and pools used in aquatic programmes for handicapped persons.

## 3.

(1)(a) The owner and the manager of any swimming pool shall not permit the use of any swimming pool in his premises without having previously registered in writing, on the appropriate form, the said pool with the competent authority.

(b) Particulars and other information given on the registration form shall not be misleading, inaccurate and incomplete.

(c) The competent authority shall keep a register of all swimming pools registered.

(2) Registration of swimming pools under this regulation shall not exempt the owner and or manager from any obligation to apply for and acquire the necessary licences or permits that may be required by any competent authority.

## 4. Amending the records.

It shall be the duty of the responsible person to inform the competent authority of any change in the information given on the registration form within twenty-eight days from such occurrence.

## 5. Pool safety operating procedure manual.

The responsible person shall:

(a) establish and make available to the competent authority a pool safety operating procedure manual, which should include normal procedures addressing injury and accident prevention strategies. It should also include the daily pool operation details, risks assessment, a plan of the pool outlining hazards, access points, information points and supervision requirements;

*Water quality monitoring.*

(b) provide evidence to the competent authority that the water quality of the swimming pool has always been kept within the recommended levels for the parameters listed in Schedule I and II;

(c) keep relevant records of all monitoring results which may be carried out;

(d) report to the competent authority any results which may indicate that the water quality has exceeded the recommended levels in Schedules I and II;

(e) report to the competent authority and register any cases of infections and, or accidents;

(f) comply with any conditions or guidelines, which the competent authority may issue from time to time.

## 6. Heated swimming pools.

The responsible person of any heated swimming pool shall:

*Amended by: L.N. 135 of 2008.*

(a) keep the pool water temperature between 27ºC and 30ºC at all times:

Provided that in the case of pools used for hydrotherapy the temperature of the pool water shall be between 28ºC to 35ºC, whilst in the case of spa pools the water temperature shall not be higher than 40ºC;

(b) keep the pool hall ambient temperature always 1ºC above the water temperature at all times; and

(c) keep the relative humidity inside the pool hall at 55% to 65% RH at all times.

## 7. Pool turnover periods and maximum bathing loads.

It shall be the duty of the responsible person to operate the pools within his premises at the recommended minimum turnover periods and maximum bathing loads as indicated in Schedules III and IV respectively.

## 8. General requirements for public pools pool water clarity.

*Amended by: L.N. 135 of 2008.*

(1) At all times when the pool is in use the water shall be sufficiently clear to permit a sacchi disc or a black disc 15 centimeters in diameter on a white field, placed on the bottom of the pool at the deepest point, to be clearly visible from the pool deck.

*Bathing load.*

(2) (a) A sign indicating the maximum bathing load shall be conspicuously posted at the pool.

(b) The maximum bathing load should not be exceed at any time when the pool is in use.

*Pool water disinfection.*

(3) Pool water shall be disinfected by any method normally used for pool disinfection and by the use of any chemical listed in Schedule V.

*Decks, dressing rooms, toilet rooms, shower requirements.*

(4) (a) The dressing rooms, hallways, toilet rooms, shower rooms or other rooms to which patrons of pools have access shall be kept clean, in good repair, and well ventilated at all times.

(b) The floors of the pool deck and all shower rooms and locker rooms shall be daily treated with a 0.5% chlorine solution, or an equivalent fungicide.

(c) All persons shall bathe with warm water and soap before entering the pool. Warm water at a temperature of 50ºC (achieved within 1 minute of running the water at outlets), shall be provided at showers convenient to the pool for this purpose.

(d) Toilet, wash hand basin and shower fixtures shall be maintained in proper repair and shall be of such a number as to cater for the maximum bathing load.

*Equipment rooms and equipment.*

(e) Equipment rooms and all equipment shall be kept in good repair and in a clean and hygienic condition (f) Any persons known or suspected of having a communicable disease shall not use the pool.

## 9. Hours of use.

(a) A sign shall be conspicuously posted at the pool entrances indicating the hours of use.

(b) When the pool is not in use a CLOSED sign is to be fixed next or over the hours of use sign.

## 10. Responsibility of the competent authority.

*Amended by: L.N. 135 of 2008.*

(1) When any pool is found not to meet the requirements set by these regulations, or when a condition is found which may constitute a public health risk or a health nuisance to bathers or pool patrons, the competent authority shall order such pool to be closed.

(2) The competent authority shall also order such closure when there is significant evidence of communicable disease being transmitted through use of the pool, when the public pool is being operated in such a manner as to constitute a significant health nuisance, or when imminent public health hazards exist.

## 11. Non-applicability of regulations 3 to 9.

*Added by: L.N. 135 of 2008.*

(1) The provisions of regulations 3 to 9 shall not be applicable to swimming pools which are neither public nor commercial.

(2) It shall be the duty of the responsible person to:

(a) prove to the satisfaction of the competent authority that the pool is neither public nor commercial;

(b) ensure that the swimming pool is kept in such a state as not to pose a risk to public health.

## 12. Offences. Re-numbered by: L.N. 135 of 2008.

Any person who fails to comply with any of the provisions of these regulations shall be guilty of an offence under the Act.

## SCHEDULE III — MINIMUM POOL WATER TURNOVER PERIODS1

The purpose of giving close attention to the turnover periods is to ensure that the whole water is adequately served. Treated water must get to all parts of the pool, and polluted water must be removed, especially from areas most used and most polluted by bathers. If not, even good water treatments may not give good pool water quality.

**RECOMMENDED POOL TURNOVER PERIODS**

Conventional pools up to 25 m long with 1 m shallow end 2.5 - 3 h

Diving pools 4 - 8 h

Hydrotheraphy pools 0.5 - 1 h

Leisure water bubble pools 5 - 20 min

Leisure waters up to 0.5 m deep 10 - 45 min

Leisure waters over 1.5 m deep 2 - 2.5 h

Teaching/learning/training pools 0.5 - 1.5 h

Water slide splash pools 0.5 - 1 h

1 This is the theoretical time taken for the total pool water volume to pass through the filtration system once. It has been proven that at least 7 complete turnover periods are needed before 99% of the pool water has actually passed through the filters.

## SCHEDULE IV — MAXIMUM BATHING LOAD1

The maximum bathing load is sometimes defined as 1 bather per 3 m2. This gives a maximum bathing load for the whole pool, regardless of depth, etc., and must in any case be modified in the light of type of pool.

**RECOMMENDED BATHING LOADS**

TYPE OF POOL MAXIMUM BATHING LOAD FORMULAE

Shallow water (under 1 m) 1 bather per 2.2 m2

Standing depth water (1 - 1.5 m) 1 bather per 2.7 m2

Deep water (over 1.5 m) 1batherper4m2

The bathing load obtained using these formulae is the maximum bathing load of the pool at any one time, should not be exceeded. If the maximum bathing load is exceeded, it is considered that there will be an unacceptable risk of accidents due to bather collisions, or drowning, etc., and would have a impact on the system to maintain good water quality.
